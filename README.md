# Quantum Ringdown

Simulating quantized black hole ringdown spectra under Bekenstein–Mukhanov horizon-area quantization, and comparing them against the emission lines produced by level transitions between quantized quasinormal modes (QNMs).

## Abstract

The ringdown of a black hole following a merger is a potential candidate for revealing the signatures of quantum gravity in the emerging gravitational waves. In quantum theory, black holes are expected to have a discrete area and energy spectrum, which conflicts with the classical notion of an horizon that absorbs all infalling perturbations. We propose that the quantum black hole dissipates the energy it cannot absorb by emitting “soft” gravitons that carry away the energy difference between the energy of the infalling perturbation and the quantum transition energy. We find that the ringdown spectrum is consequently enriched with low-frequency components, and that there exists a weak low-frequency flux that persists for a timescale much longer than the ringdown itself.

The notebook:

- Loads Schwarzschild QNM frequency/damping data (scalar, vector, and gravitational perturbations) from Berti, Cardoso & Starinets.
- Computes the ladder of allowed dissipation frequencies `ω_n = ω_QNM − n·ϖ_BM` for a chosen quantization parameter α.
- Builds relative excitation/transition probability profiles for the QNM lines and the emission lines.
- Plots the combined QNM + emission spectrum, attenuated by the spacetime grey-body (transmission) factor `T(ω)`.
- Computes decay timescales for the lowest dissipation frequency as a function of α.
- Includes a from-scratch RK4 wavepacket simulation that solves the wave equation with the Zerilli effective potential to compute `T(ω)` directly, as an alternative to the provided precomputed data.

## Usage

Open `full_spectrum.ipynb` in Jupyter and run the cells in order. The notebook is organized into sections:

1. **QNM data and dissipation frequencies** — loads the QNM data and sets up the quantized dissipation spectrum.
2. **QNMs and emitted radiation spectrum** — normalizes and plots the QNM and emission spectral lines, weighted by the grey-body transmission factor (`images/QNM_freqs.png`, `images/grey_spectrum.png`).
3. **Timescales for different BM quanta** — computes the decay timescale of the lowest dissipation frequency as a function of the Bekenstein–Mukhanov parameter α (`images/timescales.png`).
4. **Spacetime grey-body factor** — (optional, slow) recomputes `transmission.npz` from scratch via RK4 wavepacket simulation across the Zerilli potential barrier. This step can be skipped since a precomputed file is already included; uncomment the final `np.savez(...)` line if you want to regenerate it.

## Data source

QNM frequency and damping data (`Berti_Schw/`) is from the Berti, Cardoso & Starinets ringdown tables: https://pages.jh.edu/eberti2/ringdown/

## Notes

- The RK4 wavepacket simulation (final section) can take a long time to run for a wide frequency range; the included `transmission.npz` already covers dimensionless frequencies from 1e-4 to 1.
- Default parameters correspond to a black hole mass of `M = 50 M_☉`.
